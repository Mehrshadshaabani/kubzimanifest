When to Hire a DevOps Consultant (and When Not To)

Advisory · 7 min read

"We should hire a DevOps person" is usually said the week after an incident, not as part of a plan — which means the decision that follows is often the wrong shape for the actual problem. There are three real options (do it yourself with the team you have, bring in a consultant for a bounded engagement, or hire a full-time DevOps/platform engineer), and each fits a different situation.

Do it yourself, if...

  • Your infrastructure is genuinely simple — one service, one environment, low traffic — and adding process overhead would cost more than it saves.
  • Someone on the team already has real production Kubernetes/cloud experience and has the bandwidth to own it part-time.
  • You're pre-product-market-fit and the infrastructure is going to change shape significantly in the next few months anyway.

The honest failure mode here is a team that's technically capable but stretched thin enough that "infrastructure" gets attention only during incidents — which is expensive in a different way (downtime, firefighting, security exposure) than it looks on paper.

Bring in a consultant, if...

  • The problem is bounded and well-defined: set up a cluster, migrate to Kubernetes, build a CI/CD pipeline, pass a security audit. There's a clear "done."
  • You need expertise you don't have and won't need permanently — most teams don't need a full-time Kubernetes-migration specialist after the migration is finished.
  • You want the knowledge transferred to your own team (a good consulting engagement ends with documentation and your team able to operate what was built, not a black box only the consultant understands).
  • Budget needs to be a fixed, known number rather than an open-ended salary + benefits + ramp-up commitment.

This is also where a monthly retainer (rather than a one-off project) fits teams whose infrastructure is stable but who don't have enough ongoing DevOps work to justify a full-time hire — patching, monitoring, and incident response on a predictable monthly cost, without a hiring process.

Hire full-time, if...

  • Infrastructure work is now continuous, not project-shaped — new services shipping regularly, scaling decisions weekly, not quarterly.
  • You need someone embedded in day-to-day product decisions, not just executing a defined scope.
  • The cost of a consultant's day rate, sustained indefinitely, would exceed a salary — the crossover point for most teams is somewhere around needing 15-20+ hours/week of ongoing DevOps work, consistently.

The signal that actually matters

Scope, not size, is the real decider. "We need X built and working" is a consulting engagement regardless of how big X is. "We need someone who owns infrastructure decisions going forward, indefinitely" is a hire. Most teams get this backwards by hiring too early for a bounded problem (paying a full salary for what was really a 6-week project) or waiting too long past the point where ongoing infrastructure work has clearly become a full-time job for someone.

A good first step either way: get a second opinion on what your infrastructure actually needs before committing to either path. A short consultation costs nothing and usually clarifies which of the three categories you're actually in.
